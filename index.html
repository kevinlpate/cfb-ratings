Kalshi spreads vs Vegas

For each game, the Kalshi spread market ("wins by over X points") priced furthest below its fair price, where fair comes from the current Vegas spread and accounts for key numbers: college football margins pile up on 3, 7, 10, 14, 17 and 21, so a half point across 3 or 7 is worth far more than one across 5. Gap after fee = fair price minus Kalshi's buy price and taker fee, in cents per $1 contract. Our cover % for the same bet is context only: tested against closing spreads from 2017 on, our projected margin has added nothing beyond the Vegas line, so the fair price comes from Vegas, not from us. The Vegas line is the latest one pulled and can lag a moving market; thin Kalshi markets (little traded) may not fill at the price shown. Research comparison, not betting advice.

Resume rankings

What each team has done, not how good it is. Built for a top-25 ballot: the power rating on the Ratings tab answers "who would win?", this answers "who has earned it?"

PnP Resume is our top 25. It blends wins above a playoff team (70%) with Colley (30%), using only actual wins and losses, then respects head-to-head: when two teams are comparable (the winner has no more losses, the two are close on the blend, and the move is 6 spots or fewer), the winner ranks directly above the team it beat, unless that would put it above a team that beat it. "Elite team matches" is the chance a top-4 team would match the record against the same schedule; lower is harder. The other columns are the pieces, so you can check every spot.

Wins above a playoff team = actual wins minus the wins a playoff-caliber team (our No. —) would expect against the same schedule, at the same sites. Opponent strength comes from our ratings; the team's own rating plays no part, and margin doesn't matter, only wins and losses. "Playoff team matches" is the chance that team would do as well or better: lower is a harder record to earn. Colley is the classic computer ranking from wins and losses alone, adjusted for schedule, with no margin, site or preseason opinion. Schedule rank = how many losses a playoff team would expect against it (No. 1 is the hardest). Rating vs resume = power-rating rank minus resume rank: positive means the resume is better than the team. FCS opponents count, as one pooled FCS team. Ranks of best wins and losses are power-rating ranks.

Deserved versions take the luck out. Deserved wins = for each game, the chance a team wins when it plays the way it did (its play-by-play efficiency margin, with garbage time removed; the scoreboard and site play no part), added up over the season. Luck = actual wins minus deserved wins: +2 means about two more wins than the play earned. Deserved resume and deserved Colley rebuild both rankings from those deserved results. Composite = the average of all four ranks. Some of what looks like luck is real: since 2015, teams that won more than their play earned kept beating expectations a little, from things efficiency misses such as special teams and finishing drives. So read luck as "this record flatters them", not as a prediction.

Player values

Each position is valued the way it held up best when we tested it on later seasons, in points per game. Quarterbacks: expected points added per play (passes, sacks and runs), shrunk toward the league average until he has about 150 plays, minus what a backup-level quarterback produces, times his plays per game. Archetype comes from how he plays (how often he runs, sacks and throwaways, volume), not how well. Running backs: the model's estimate of expected points per carry above an average back after taking out his offensive line and play-calling (the offense's own effect) and the defenses he faced, times carries per game. Receivers: there is no reliable skill number for receivers (their EPA is mostly the quarterback and the offense), so value here is the EPA on passes thrown his way per game, a description of production, not a rating. Target share and air yards per target (aDOT) are the steadier signals. YAC = yards after the catch per catch; CPOE = completion percentage over expected on his targets. Qualifying: 100+ QB plays, 50+ carries, 25+ targets. FBS teams only.

How the ratings are built, and how good they are

Backtested on every game between FBS teams from 2017 through 2025 that had a closing line, each predicted only from information available before kickoff.

The ratings are a Bayesian model. Each team's strength is an estimate with a known uncertainty. It starts the season from the preseason rating (roster talent, the coach's record against his talent, returning production) blended with how the team finished last season, and widens a little each week because teams change.

Every game then updates both teams twice over: once from the final score (with lopsided blowouts trimmed) and once from the play-by-play efficiency margin, the expected points each offense added. The model learns from past seasons how much to trust each reading, how fast the preseason rating should give way to results, and how much home field is worth (about 2.8 points this season; it's re-tuned before each season on the previous three). Because beating a team that later proves good keeps paying off, every rating is adjusted for the whole schedule, FCS opponents included.

The Vegas closing line is the bar. On the 2023–2025 seasons, which the model never saw while it was being tuned, its average miss was 0.35 points per game larger than the close, and 0.1 late in the season.
